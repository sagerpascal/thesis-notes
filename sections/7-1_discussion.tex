\section{The task fixes what is computed and the procedure how far it spreads}
\label{sec:disc_levels}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The results of this thesis separate two levels at which a question about a
trained network can be asked. At one level the question is what the network
computes, and for modular addition the answer is settled: every rule that
generalises arrives at the same Fourier construction. The universality claim says exactly that, and \cref{chap:grokking} confirms it. At the other level the question is how that computation is
spread across the parts available to it, and there the answer is not settled by the
task. The same algorithm is implemented by about $154$ neurons or by about
$254$ at width $256$, depending on the rule that trained the network.

Nobody had claimed otherwise, and presenting this as a refutation would
overstate it. The literature read the concentrated solutions it found as what
the task offers, and did so on evidence from networks that were all trained
the same way. The comparison that would have settled it had not been run.

The separation is not peculiar to neurons. \Cref{chap:attention} holds the
credit assignment algorithm fixed and varies the objective, and finds that the
objective settles whether a Transformer's attention weights stay close to
symmetric, at a scale where the question can be asked of models people use.
\Cref{chap:bounded} takes the limiting case, a quantity that a loss comparing
directions alone leaves unconstrained, and shows what a measurement of it then
reports. Together these say that different components of the
training setup settle different structural properties, and that the objective
settles none of them directly. Which component settled a property has to be
established before that property is read as a fact about the task.

\section{The structural measure reports the direction of the central result and overstates its level}
\label{sec:disc_standard}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\Cref{chap:bounded} argues that a readout has to be shown to respond to the
quantity it names before its ranking means anything, and offers four checks
for it. The measure at the centre of this thesis is itself a readout: the
participation ratio of a layer's outgoing weight norms is a number computed
from weights, read as a statement about computation. It would be inconsistent
to set that standard in one chapter and exempt the measure in another, so the
checks are applied here, and the result is mixed.

The free quantity in this case is a rescaling. Multiplying a neuron's incoming
weights by a positive constant and dividing its outgoing weights by the same
constant leaves the function unchanged and moves the outgoing norm, so the
measure has a degree of freedom the computation does not see. That is the same
shape of problem as a latent whose norm drifts while its direction stays
accurate.

Of the four checks, one does not transfer. The support check asks whether a
prediction's direction lies among the directions the encoder produces, and
there is no analogue for a weight norm. The other three do.

The check that asks how much the reported quantity carries beyond a measure
immune to the free direction is the expensive one. Measures tied to the
computation, which weight a neuron by what it contributes on real inputs or by
what is lost when it is removed, place the untouched network at about $0.34$
and $0.42$ where the structural measure places it at about $0.60$. Under the intervention they rise to about $0.81$ and $0.80$. The check
therefore confirms the direction of the result and puts the level in doubt.
The headline reads that the intervention raises every measure of
spread, with the structural one giving the largest numbers.

The check on agreement between readouts is passed. The three participation
measures, structural, contribution weighted and ablation weighted, move the
same way under credit equalisation. Under the imposed profiles only the
structural measure and the deletion tolerance curve are reported, and those
two move together.

The check on null controls is passed. A credit profile imposed under a random
permutation moves the circuit while having nothing to do with what any neuron
computes. One rule settles the gauge question outright. Nero holds each
neuron's incoming weight norm fixed, so it cannot move scale between the two
sides of a neuron, and it reaches participation $0.986$ at test accuracy
$0.985$ without the intervention at all.

The structural measure therefore overstates the level while reporting the
direction correctly, and that is visible only because the chapter reports the
other two measures alongside it. A reader comparing circuit sizes across papers should ask which
measure produced the number.

One question the checks do not settle sits in that chapter's own appendix. A
network trained with decoupled decay has nearly equal outgoing norms, which
the structural measure calls distributed, and it prunes to about as few
neurons as the concentrated reference. The structural measure therefore
separates a pair of networks that the pruning treats alike. Participation and
minimal circuit size are different
quantities, and which of them a claim about spread should rest on is open.

\section{The rule, an imposed profile or the architecture can each set the structure}
\label{sec:disc_design}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

If a structural property is settled by the procedure and has a price, then it
is a thing to be chosen. This thesis leaves three places where the choice can
be made, of which the first two concern the circuit it measures and the third
a different structure reached by the same mechanism.

The first is to choose the rule. The condition of \cref{chap:grokking} answers
with a yes or a no for each rule and supplies no ranking among them, and the
answer is that only a normaliser shared by exactly one neuron moves the
circuit. This is the cheapest route and the most constrained, because it
offers whichever circuits the available rules happen to produce.

The second is to impose the circuit and then establish that the imposition did
what it claims. The imposed profiles of \cref{chap:grokking} are an existence
proof that the circuit can be set directly, and the standard of
\cref{chap:bounded} distinguishes a circuit that was set from a measurement
artefact. No experiment runs across the two chapters.

The third is to build the structure into the architecture, which is
\cref{chap:cna}. There a neuron is part of a representation only while others hold it there,
so the redundancy sits in the architecture and no regulariser produces it.

A structural parallel between two of the chapters is recorded here as an
observation, with nothing resting on it. A Hebbian update changes the connection
between two neurons by a quantity symmetric in the two, so repeated
application accumulates a symmetric weight matrix. That is the same mechanism
\cref{chap:attention} identifies in bidirectional training, where each pair of
tokens enters the update in both orders and the antisymmetric parts cancel.
Both arrive at symmetric weights for the same reason: their updates are sums over
unordered pairs.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limitations}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The central result is established in one setting and the price is measured
only there. The condition governs unused units in a language model of $30$
million parameters, but nothing at that scale shows the circuit to be a
quantity with a measured cost in robustness, pruning and transfer. Those
measurements exist for modular addition and for a handful of related tasks,
and claiming them at scale would be claiming more than was run.

The reference setting of \cref{chap:grokking} is one the field has largely
moved away from. Adding an L2 penalty to the gradient before an adaptive
optimizer produces the concentration most directly, and under decoupled weight
decay the untouched reference already spreads its computation across most of
the settings that chapter measures. The general claim, that a neuron drops out when its credit
is small relative to a non gradient term of its update, survives that change,
while the claim that the condition explains what is left fails. Its other two terms dominate only where the decay reaches a network's
normalisation parameters, and where it does not the untouched network still
moves from $0.82$ to $0.91$ under the intervention, which the paper's
limitations say the condition does not account for. The headline numbers
belong to the coupled setting and should be quoted with it.

The measure overstates the level, as \cref{sec:disc_standard} sets out, and
the effect of the intervention depends on the width. At width $32$ the
untouched network already uses $30$ of its $32$ neurons, and on a vision task
and a canonical transformer there is nothing for the intervention to do. The
finding is about how much of the available width gets used, and a network
whose width is already fully used is outside its scope.

\Cref{chap:attention} establishes its mechanism by derivation and verifies it
in matched twelve layer models. The comparison across released models is
observational, since those models differ in more than their objective. Part of
what distinguishes the two objectives in a decoder is the attention mask,
which belongs to the architecture, so the clean statement concerns the set of
token pairs that enter the update, with the loss function being only part of
what fixes that set.

\Cref{chap:bounded} reproduces a manuscript that is not yet submitted. Its
synthetic analysis is exact and its demonstration on published models covers
one family of decoder free world models. Its four checks are proposed here and
have not been used by anyone else.

\Cref{chap:cna} is a proposal. Its features are hand designed, its
demonstrations are on line drawings, it defines no dynamics over a
representation, and the code behind it no longer runs, so the measurements
that would connect it to \cref{chap:grokking} cannot now be made.

Finally, no single measurement runs across the chapters. Two were planned, participation over the attention
weights of pretrained models and neuron deletion on the nets of
\cref{chap:cna}, and neither was made. The second cannot be made now, because
the code no longer runs. The first remains possible, on models anyone can download, and the time to do
it ran out. A standard
and an argument about a shared mechanism cross the chapters, which falls short
of a shared measurement.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:disc_outlook}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The question this thesis asks has an older form in neuroscience, where it is
asked of populations of cells and not of a trained layer, and where the
participation ratio used here comes from. The analogy is limited in the three
respects \cref{sec:bg_sparsedistributed} names. Where learning is local and no
global error is propagated, nothing plays the role that credit assignment
plays here, and the question of what does play it is open.

The nearer prospect is the one \cref{chap:cna} opens. A representation in
which the parts of a pattern are explicit and support one another is close to
what an agent needs as the state of a model of its environment, which is the
third of the gaps \cref{chap:acu} identifies. To serve that purpose it would
need features learned from data, a demonstration beyond line drawings, and dynamics defined over the representation so that a
successor state can be predicted from a current one and an action. Such a
predictor would have to be evaluated with the probe correction of
\cref{chap:bounded}, and the condition of \cref{chap:grokking} would decide
how much of it ends up carrying the work. Building that system is left to
later work. This thesis supplies two of the pieces it would need, a condition
on the rule that would set its circuit and a standard for the readout that
would score it.

\section{A task fixes what a network computes, not how the computation is laid out}
\label{sec:intro_layout}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A neural network that has learned a task has found one of many parameter
settings that solve it. These settings compute the same function on the
training data and reach the same loss. They differ in where the computation
sits. It can be carried by a few of the network's neurons while the rest stay
idle, or it can be spread across all of them. Inside a weight matrix it can
concentrate on a few rows or columns, or spread evenly over both. The way a
computation is distributed over a network's parts, whether those parts are
neurons or the rows and columns of a weight matrix, is what this thesis calls
the solution's \emph{layout}.

How a computation should be laid out across neurons is an old question.
Neuroscience has argued for decades about sparse against distributed
coding~\cite{barlow_single_1972,hinton_distributed_1986,olshausen_emergence_1996}.
A distributed code degrades gracefully when neurons are lost, because no single
neuron is essential. A sparse code interferes less when new associations are
learned, because fewer neurons are shared between them. Population coding makes
the first half quantitative: the more neurons carry a signal, the smaller the
fluctuation when some of them are removed~\cite{averbeck_neural_2006}.

Machine learning has mostly not asked the question, because it has assumed
that the task decides the layout. The clearest case is a network that fits its
training data perfectly, sits at chance on held out data for thousands of
epochs, and then generalises abruptly, a delay the literature calls
grokking~\cite{power_grokking_2022}. For modular addition the generalising
solution has been worked out neuron by neuron, and it is sparse, meaning a few
neurons carry the computation~\cite{nanda_progress_2023}. That sparsity is
read as what the task demands. The strongest form of this reading is the claim
of universality, that every network solving a task finds the same circuit, so
that a circuit found in one network stands for the task
itself~\cite{chughtai_toy_2023}. Every network behind that evidence was trained
with backpropagation.

This thesis shows that the layout of a learned solution is decided by the
procedure that trained the network, and not by the task. The layout can be read
off the update equation of that procedure without running it. It can be
chosen. And the choice has a price. Solutions spread over many neurons survive
damage better, and solutions concentrated on a few neurons adapt to a new task
faster. The thesis closes with a proposal for representations in which a
distributed, mutually supporting layout is built into the architecture rather
than left to the training procedure.

The procedure has two parts that this thesis keeps apart. The computation that
assigns each weight its share of the error at the output is the
\emph{credit assignment algorithm}, and backpropagation is one such algorithm.
The procedure that turns those shares into a change of the weights, such as
stochastic gradient descent or Adam, is the \emph{optimizer}. The pair of the
two is the \emph{learning rule}. The training objective is a third choice, and
Chapter~\ref{chap:attention} shows that it decides a layout as well.

\section{Agents are built by one recipe, and what they lack are properties of the trained network}
\label{sec:intro_agents}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The stake is set by agents for computer use, systems that carry out tasks on a
computer or a phone from an instruction in natural language, by controlling
software through clicks, keystrokes and gestures. Chapter~\ref{chap:acu}
surveys $87$ research papers and $33$ datasets in this
field~\cite{sager_survey_2026}. The field is converging on one recipe: a
pretrained foundation model, adapted by prompting or by behaviour cloning from
human demonstrations, observing the screen as an image. Agents built this way
share six gaps. Two of them are properties of the trained network rather than
of the task or the benchmark. The first is insufficient generalisation to
interfaces and task formulations not seen in training. The second is
inefficient learning, meaning large demonstration sets and poor transfer to
related tasks. The survey names these properties. It does not say what decides
them, and the rest of the thesis asks that question of the recipe.

Two shorter studies, in which the thesis author had a limited role, show the
same recipe from two more sides. A benchmark of methods for running large
language models on limited hardware finds that quantisation, advertised as
nearly lossless, produces a loss in text quality that human raters notice and
the standard metrics miss~\cite{tuggener_private_2024}. A framework for
compositional visual reasoning finds that architectures with the inductive
biases thought to support composition still fail on rule combinations not seen
in training~\cite{cogitao}. Both are in Chapter~\ref{chap:llmbench}.

\section{The learning rule decides the layout, and the layout has a price}
\label{sec:intro_rule}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} asks the question in the grokking setting, because
there the solution can be read at the level of single
neurons~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}. Task, data, architecture, optimizer and
weight decay are held fixed, and only the learning rule varies, across twelve
published rules and a family of rules built for the purpose. Four of the twelve
never generalise. Their update does not vanish at the minima of the loss, so
training comes to rest away from those minima, and away from the solution that
weight decay would otherwise select. Among the rules that generalise, loss,
margin and weight norm agree, and the number of neurons carrying the solution
runs from about $154$ to $254$ of $256$.

The chapter then moves the layout on purpose. A rescaling of each neuron's
share of the error that provably leaves the objective and every neuron's update
direction unchanged drives that number across its whole range. It does so
whether applied from the start or switched on after the network has
generalised, so the layout is a state the rule maintains rather than one locked
in at the transition. Which published rules spread the solution can be read
from their update equation. Only rules that normalise the update one neuron at
a time do so. A normaliser shared by a group of neurons leaves the inequality
within the group untouched, and a model small enough to solve exactly derives
the whole curve from that fact. The prediction places all eight published
optimizers that were tested.

The price is proven and measured. Deleting a random fraction of neurons
removes, on average, the same fraction of every solution's summed
contributions. What separates solutions is the fluctuation around that
average, and it falls as one over the square root of the effective number of
neurons. Spread solutions survive random and targeted deletion far better.
Concentrated solutions adapt to a new task about twice as fast, in part because
they leave idle neurons that a new task can claim. Neither end is better, and
choosing a rule is choosing between the two.

Chapter~\ref{chap:attention} asks the same question one level up, of the
weights that carry attention in a Transformer, with backpropagation fixed and
the training objective varied~\cite{saponati_structures_2025}. The product of
the query and key matrices is one bilinear form, and its update is a sum over
the pairs of tokens the objective lets one token use to predict another. An
autoregressive objective uses each token as context for every later token but
as target only once, so a few columns of the form come to dominate. A
bidirectional objective enters every pair in both orders, so the update is
symmetric and the structure spreads over rows and columns alike. Both layouts
appear in ModernBERT, GPT, LLaMA3 and Mistral, and across text, vision and
audio. Initialising the form symmetrically speeds up the training of
bidirectional models. The thesis author designed and ran most of the
experiments of that paper. The mathematical framework is Matteo Saponati's,
and the contribution statement of the chapter says so.

\section{Structure supplied from outside the data must be measured against a predictor that learns nothing}
\label{sec:intro_outside}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The layout results rest on controls. Every comparison is made at matched loss,
margin and weight norm, and against trivial constructions such as a credit
profile dealt to neurons at random. Chapter~\ref{chap:bounded} shows what
happens without such controls, in a study of latent world models, meaning
models that predict the future state of an environment in a learned latent
space~\cite{sager_bounded_2026}. The prediction loss is invariant to the scale
of the latent, so training does not fix that scale. A spectral constraint on
the transition operator fixes how the scale grows under repeated prediction,
and the standard readout, a linear probe fitted at the training scale, reads
exactly that growth. The constrained model wins by five orders of magnitude
under the probe and by nothing under a readout that ignores scale. Predictors
that learn no dynamics beat every learned model. The constraint does what its
theory says, and the inference from a bounded error curve to a better model
does not follow.

Two further chapters supply structure from outside the data and pass their
controls. Chapter~\ref{chap:hounsfield} adds binary masks derived from the
Hounsfield unit ranges of tissue types to the input of a CT segmentation
model, which improves accuracy and matches the baseline with half of the
training data~\cite{meyer_hounsfield_2025}. Chapter~\ref{chap:retrieval}
retrieves scientific sources for social media posts with a pipeline that
combines lexical search, dense retrieval and a cross encoder, placing first on
the development set and third of $31$ teams on the test set of a shared task
without external training data~\cite{sager_deep_2025}. These three chapters
are shorter than the others, and each opens with the thesis author's role.

\section{A layout fixed by construction is the proposal}
\label{sec:intro_proposal}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

If the rule decides the layout and the layout has a price, the layout can
instead be fixed in the architecture, and learning can be left to fill in its
parts. Chapter~\ref{chap:cna} presents the Cooperative Network Architecture as
a first implementation of that proposal~\cite{sager_cooperative_2025}. It
represents a sensory pattern by a set of neurons each of which is supported by
several others through recurrent connections, assembled from fragments learned
by Hebbian plasticity~\cite{hebb_organization_1949}. Support is built in, so a
neuron stays active only when the pattern around it is one the network has
seen often. Noise has no such support and is filtered even when more than half
of the input is corrupted. Missing parts of a figure receive support from
their surroundings and are completed. Fragments learned on straight lines
recombine into digits, characters and line drawings that never appeared in
training. The implementation does not yet scale beyond line drawings, uses
fixed feature detectors, and needs a hand set attenuation schedule. The chapter
states these limits, and its summary lists the steps from this representation
to one that an agent could plan over.

\section{Research questions}
\label{sec:intro_questions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Four questions organise the thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Is the layout of a learned solution fixed by the task, or by the
    procedure that trained the network? Chapters~\ref{chap:grokking}
    and~\ref{chap:attention} answer this by varying one part of the procedure
    at a time with everything else held fixed.
    \item Can the layout be predicted from the training rule without running
    it, and can it be chosen? Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} derives the
    prediction from the update equation and tests it on published optimizers,
    and Chapter~\ref{chap:attention} does the same for the training objective.
    \item What does a layout cost? Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} proves and
    measures the trade between robustness to deletion and speed of adaptation.
    \item Given that the rule decides the layout and the layout has a price,
    can the layout be fixed by construction instead, and what would that buy?
    Chapter~\ref{chap:cna} answers with a proposal and a first implementation.
\end{enumerate}

Chapter~\ref{chap:acu} frames why the first three questions matter for agents.
Chapters~\ref{chap:bounded} to~\ref{chap:retrieval} supply applied instances
of structure from outside the data and one cautionary measurement. None of
these tests the questions, and the thesis does not claim that they do.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro_contributions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This is a cumulative thesis. Each results chapter is built around one paper,
reproduced as published, and the thesis author's role is stated at the opening
of each chapter. The contributions, in the order the thesis presents them, are
these.

\paragraph{A survey and taxonomy of agents for computer use.} Shared first
authorship with Benjamin Meyer. A taxonomy along the domain, interaction and
agent perspectives, applied to $87$ papers and $33$ datasets, and six
research gaps derived from the classification~\cite{sager_survey_2026}. The
reading of two of those gaps as properties of the trained network is thesis
work.

\paragraph{The learning rule decides the layout of a grokked solution.} First
authorship. Twelve published rules compared at a matched setting, a criterion
for which rules generalise at all, a per neuron rescaling of credit that moves
the layout while provably leaving the objective and every update direction
unchanged, a law that predicts from a rule's update equation whether it spreads
the solution, and the trade between deletion tolerance and adaptation speed,
proven in a tractable model and measured~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}. The paper
also shows grokking under a control driven learning rule, to our knowledge for
the first time.

\paragraph{The training objective decides the layout of the attention
weights.} Shared first authorship with Matteo Saponati, who developed the
mathematical framework. The thesis author designed and ran most of the
experiments across model families and modalities. Bidirectional training
induces symmetry in the query key form and autoregressive training induces
column dominance, and symmetric initialisation speeds up bidirectional
training~\cite{saponati_structures_2025}.

\paragraph{The Cooperative Network Architecture.} First authorship. The first
formal definition of nets and net fragments, a computational framework that
learns fragments by Hebbian plasticity, and the evidence that the resulting
representation filters noise, completes figures and recombines fragments into
unseen patterns~\cite{sager_cooperative_2025}. Its reading as a
layout fixed by construction, and the proposal built on it, are thesis work.

\paragraph{Three shorter studies.} A controlled study of latent world models
in which a constraint that wins by five orders of magnitude under the standard
readout is shown to be a measurement artefact, with the protocol that catches
it, first authorship~\cite{sager_bounded_2026}. Hounsfield unit masks as an
input constraint for data efficient CT segmentation, shared first authorship
with Benjamin Meyer, with the thesis author responsible for the framing rather
than the implementation~\cite{meyer_hounsfield_2025}. A hybrid retrieval
pipeline for scientific source retrieval, first
authorship~\cite{sager_deep_2025}. In the benchmark of efficient language
models the thesis author ran the model evaluation~\cite{tuggener_private_2024},
and in the compositional reasoning framework the thesis author evaluated one
of the baseline models~\cite{cogitao}.

\paragraph{The frame.} The definition of layout, the connection between the
grokking, attention and CNA results, the reading of the survey's gaps as
properties of the trained network, and the proposal of Part~V are the thesis
author's work and appear in no paper.

\section{Structure of the thesis}
\label{sec:intro_structure}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Chapter~\ref{chap:background} gives the background the later chapters rest
on: sparse and distributed coding, the implicit bias of training procedures
and the quantities gradient flow conserves, grokking and its circuits, self
attention as a bilinear form, credit assignment rules other than
backpropagation, Hebbian assemblies, and the evaluation of latent world
models.

The thesis then has five parts. Agents Built by One Recipe holds the survey
and the two shorter studies of the recipe. The Training Procedure Decides the
Layout holds the grokking and the attention chapters. Structure Supplied from
Outside the Data holds the world model, Hounsfield and retrieval chapters. A
Layout Fixed by Construction holds the Cooperative Network Architecture and
the proposal. Synthesis holds the discussion, which states what the task fixes
and what the procedure fixes and what follows for the design of learning
rules and architectures, and the conclusion.

Every paper backed chapter has the same shape. A box gives the full citation
and the status of the published paper. A contribution statement names the
co-authors and the thesis author's share. A chapter introduction, written for
this thesis, connects the previous chapter's result to this one and gives the
mapping between the paper's notation and the thesis notation where they
differ. The body of the paper follows as published. A chapter summary, written
for this thesis, states the chapter's findings once in full and hands off to
the next chapter. The thesis frame uses British spelling. The imported
chapters keep the spelling they were published with.

Two networks can fit the same data equally well and still be built differently
inside. One carries its computation on a few of its neurons and leaves the rest
idle. The other spreads the same computation over all of them. Both reach the
same training loss, so nothing in the data chooses between them. Something
does, and this thesis is about what.

It matters which of the two a network becomes, because the two fail in
different ways. A computation spread over many neurons survives the loss of any
one of them, since none is essential. A computation held on a few can be
repurposed for a new task without disturbing much of what is already there.
Robustness and adaptability are among the properties that deployed systems are
most often found to lack, and they turn out to be the two ends of this single
axis rather than two independent goals.

Machine learning has treated the position on that axis as something the task
settles. This thesis shows that the training procedure settles it, that its
choice can be read off the procedure's update equation before the procedure is
ever run, and that neither end of the axis is free. It closes by arguing that a
property with consequences this large should be designed rather than inherited.

\section{A task fixes what a network computes, not how the computation is laid out}
\label{sec:intro_layout}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A neural network that has learned a task has found one of many parameter
settings that solve it. Those settings compute the same function on the
training data and reach the same loss. What separates them is where the
computation sits. It can be carried by a few of the network's neurons while the
rest stay idle, or spread across all of them. Inside a weight matrix it can
concentrate on a few rows or columns, or spread evenly over both. The way a
computation is distributed over a network's parts, whether those parts are
neurons or the rows and columns of a weight matrix, is what this thesis calls
the solution's \emph{layout}.

Neuroscience has argued about the right layout for
decades~\cite{barlow_single_1972,hinton_distributed_1986,olshausen_emergence_1996}.
The argument is not aesthetic, because each end buys something the other
cannot. A distributed code degrades gracefully under the loss of neurons, and
population coding makes that precise, since the more neurons carry a signal the
smaller the fluctuation when some are removed~\cite{averbeck_neural_2006}. A
sparse code interferes less when something new is learned, because fewer
neurons are shared between what is stored.

Machine learning has largely not asked the question, because it has assumed the
task answers it. The clearest case is grokking, where a network fits its
training data perfectly, sits at chance on held out data for thousands of
epochs, and then generalises abruptly~\cite{power_grokking_2022}. For modular
addition the generalising solution has been read neuron by neuron and is
sparse, meaning a few neurons carry the computation~\cite{nanda_progress_2023}.
That sparsity is taken to be what the task demands, and the strongest version
of the reading is universality, the claim that every network solving a task
finds the same circuit, so that one network's circuit stands for the
task~\cite{chughtai_toy_2023}. Every network behind that evidence was trained
with backpropagation, which is one procedure among many. The evidence therefore
cannot separate what the task requires from what that one procedure produces,
and separating them is what this thesis does.

Three choices inside the procedure are kept apart throughout, because the
thesis varies them one at a time. The computation that assigns each weight its
share of the error at the output is the \emph{credit assignment algorithm}, and
backpropagation is one such algorithm. The procedure that turns those shares
into a change of the weights, such as stochastic gradient descent or Adam, is
the \emph{optimizer}. The pair of the two is the \emph{learning rule}. The
training objective is the third choice, and Section~\ref{sec:intro_objective}
shows that it fixes a layout of its own.

\section{The gaps that motivate the question are properties of the trained network}
\label{sec:intro_agents}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The question did not start as a question about layout. It started with agents
for computer use, systems that carry out tasks on a computer or a phone from an
instruction in natural language, by controlling software through clicks,
keystrokes and gestures. Chapter~\ref{chap:acu} surveys $87$ research papers
and $33$ datasets in that field~\cite{sager_survey_2026}. The field is
converging on one recipe, a pretrained foundation model adapted by prompting or
by behaviour cloning from human demonstrations, observing the screen as an
image. Agents built that way share six gaps.

Four of the six can be closed by building harder benchmarks and agreeing on how
to score them. Two cannot, because they are properties of the trained network
rather than of the task or the measurement. The first is insufficient
generalisation to interfaces and task formulations not seen in training. The
second is inefficient learning, meaning large demonstration sets and poor
transfer to related tasks. Those are the two properties that
Section~\ref{sec:intro_layout} placed at opposite ends of the layout axis,
which is the connection this thesis pursues and which the survey neither shows
nor claims.

A survey cannot say what produces those two gaps, and the reason it cannot
determines what has to be done next. The field varies the model, the data and
the prompt, and it does not vary the training procedure, so the procedure
cannot appear in its results. It also measures task success, and a success rate
cannot separate two networks that score the same while being built differently
inside. Section~\ref{sec:llmbench} shows how far a score can sit from the
property it is taken to stand for. Quantisation advertised as nearly lossless
costs text quality that human raters notice and the standard metrics
miss~\cite{tuggener_private_2024}, and architectures carrying the inductive
biases thought to support composition score well in distribution and fail on
rule combinations they have not seen~\cite{cogitao}. The thesis author's role
in both was an evaluation rather than the design, and each chapter says so.

If the score does not expose the property, the property has to be measured
inside the network. That is the step from Part~\ref{part:agents} to
Part~\ref{part:procedure}, and it fixes three requirements on the study that
follows. The task must have a solution that can be read at the level of single
neurons, or a difference in layout cannot be seen at all. Everything except one
component of the procedure must be held fixed, or a difference cannot be
attributed to that component. And the networks compared must be matched on
loss, margin and weight norm, or a difference in layout can be dismissed as one
network simply fitting the data better than another.

\section{The learning rule decides the layout, and the layout has a price}
\label{sec:intro_rule}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

One setting meets all three requirements, and it is the setting the sparsity
claim came from. Grokking on modular arithmetic has a solution that has been
read neuron by neuron, it uses networks small enough to train hundreds of times
over, and weight decay drives every run to a comparable norm.
Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} therefore holds task, data, architecture, optimizer
and weight decay fixed, and varies only the learning
rule~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}.

A third of the rules never arrive. Four of twelve published rules stay at
chance on held out data however long they train, and what separates them from
the rest is where training comes to rest. A rule whose update still pushes
somewhere at the minima of the loss has different stationary points than the
loss has, so it cannot stop where gradient descent stops. The displacement is
largest along the directions the loss leaves flat, and those are the directions
the layout lives in, which is why such a rule fits the training data and never
finds the generalising solution. Whether a rule reaches that solution can
therefore be decided in advance by asking whether the part of its update that
is not the gradient fades as the loss falls.

Among the rules that do arrive, the layout is not the same twice. Loss, margin
and weight norm agree across them, and the number of neurons carrying the
solution runs from about $154$ to $254$ of $256$. The task offers every rule
the same set of solutions and each rule stops at a different member of it, so
the sparse circuit that the literature reads as what modular arithmetic demands
is what backpropagation produces on modular arithmetic.

Two rules differ in many ways at once, so that comparison shows a difference
without showing its cause. Isolating the cause needs an intervention that
changes how credit is divided between neurons and changes nothing else, and the
chapter builds one. A rescaling of each neuron's share of the error provably
leaves the objective untouched and leaves every neuron's update direction
untouched, altering only how much of the total each neuron receives. It moves
the count across the whole range. It does so whether it is applied from the
first step or switched on after the network has already generalised, which
answers a second question at the same time. A layout that can be reorganised
after the fact is a state the rule holds in place, not a state locked in at the
moment of generalisation.

An intervention built for a purpose invites the objection that it demonstrates
only itself, so the next question is whether rules in ordinary use behave the
same way. They do, and which of them move the layout can be read from their
update equation without training any of them. A normaliser shared by a group of
neurons rescales the whole group by one factor and therefore cannot alter the
inequality between the neurons inside it, so only a rule that normalises one
neuron at a time can move the layout at all. A model small enough to solve
exactly turns that observation into the shape of the whole curve, and the
prediction places all eight published optimizers that were tested. One of them
is the sharpest case, because it spreads credit perfectly evenly and still
leaves the layout concentrated, its normaliser being per parameter rather than
per neuron.

What remains is whether the layout is worth choosing, and it is, in both
directions. Deleting neurons at random costs every solution the same expected
fraction of its summed contributions, so what separates solutions is the
fluctuation around that average, and that fluctuation falls as one over the
square root of the effective number of units. Solutions spread over many
neurons therefore survive random and targeted deletion far better. Concentrated
solutions adapt to a new task about twice as fast, partly because they leave
idle neurons a new task can claim without disturbing working structure. Neither
end is better than the other. Choosing a learning rule is choosing between the
two properties that Section~\ref{sec:intro_agents} identified as the survey's
two structural gaps.

\section{The training objective decides a layout of its own}
\label{sec:intro_objective}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Three things about that result invite doubt. It rests on networks of a few
hundred neurons, on one family of tasks, and on an intervention constructed for
the experiment. All three are answered by asking the same question about a
component the field does vary, in an architecture built at scale, while holding
the credit assignment at backpropagation. Chapter~\ref{chap:attention} does
that with the training objective~\cite{saponati_structures_2025}.

The object of the analysis is the product of a Transformer's query and key
matrices, which is the one bilinear form through which the two ever act. Its
update is a sum over the pairs of tokens the objective lets one token use to
predict another, and the two standard objectives differ in exactly which pairs
those are. Autoregressive training uses each token as context for every later
token and as a target only once, so a few columns of the form accumulate most
of the update and come to dominate it. Bidirectional training enters every pair
in both orders, so the update is symmetric and the structure spreads over rows
and columns alike. Both layouts are found in ModernBERT, GPT, LLaMA3 and
Mistral, and across text, vision and audio.

The result answers the three doubts and pays for itself twice. It is not a
property of small networks, because it holds in models with billions of
parameters. It is not an artefact of an intervention of our own, because the
objectives compared are the ones the field already uses. Initialising the form
symmetrically follows from the derivation and speeds up the training of
bidirectional models, which is the layout being chosen deliberately for the
first time in this thesis. And the argument closes back on
Section~\ref{sec:intro_agents}, because autoregressive prediction is the
objective behind every foundation model the survey found, so the agents whose
gaps opened this chain carry a layout their objective imposed on them.

The thesis author designed and ran most of the experiments of that paper. The
mathematical framework is Matteo Saponati's, and the contribution statement of
the chapter says so in detail.

\section{A property the procedure fixes can be mistaken for one the model learned}
\label{sec:intro_outside}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Together the two preceding chapters say that a training procedure can fix a
structural property of a network that the data leaves open. That creates a
hazard for measurement. If a readout is sensitive to a property the procedure
fixes on its own, the procedure can satisfy the readout with nothing having
been learned, and the resulting number will look like evidence about the model.

Chapter~\ref{chap:bounded} is where this thesis walked into
that~\cite{sager_bounded_2026}. The study began on the third of the survey's
gaps, since planning over several steps needs a model that predicts several
steps ahead, and the standard remedy for the compounding error of such a model
is to bound the spectrum of its transition operator. The comparison was built
to be clean, with one encoder and one objective and four dynamics modules
differing only in that bound. The bounded operator wins by five orders of
magnitude under the readout this literature uses, a linear probe from the
latent space to the physical state.

Two controls that cost nothing overturn the result. Predictors that learn no
dynamics at all, one repeating the first latent and one predicting the mean
state, beat every learned model at horizons of ten steps and more. Scoring the
identical rollouts again with a readout that ignores the scale of the latent
collapses the five orders of magnitude to nothing. The mechanism explains both.
The prediction loss is invariant to the scale of the latent, so training never
fixes that scale, the bound fixes how fast it grows under rollout, and the
probe is fitted at the training scale and therefore measures exactly that
growth. Rescaling a trained operator's spectrum moves the probe curve
monotonically, and retraining with a scale aware loss removes the gap at its
source. The bound does what its theory promises. A bounded error curve is not a
better model.

This chapter is what justifies the discipline of the two before it. Every claim
about layout in Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} is made at matched loss, margin and
weight norm, and against trivial constructions, among them a credit profile
drawn to a chosen shape and dealt to neurons at random, and backpropagation's
own credit magnitudes permuted between neurons at every step. Those controls
exist because a procedure that can fix a structure can also fix a measurement,
and the only defence is a comparison the procedure alone cannot win.

Structure supplied from outside the data is not discredited by any of this, and
two further studies supply it and survive the same controls.
Section~\ref{sec:hounsfield} adds binary masks derived from the Hounsfield
unit ranges of tissue types to the input of a CT segmentation model, which
improves accuracy against the same model without masks and matches the baseline
with half of the training data~\cite{meyer_hounsfield_2025}.
Section~\ref{sec:retrieval} retrieves scientific sources for social media
posts with a pipeline combining lexical search, dense retrieval and a cross
encoder, placing first on the development set and third of $31$ teams on the
test set of a shared task without external training
data~\cite{sager_deep_2025}. Both are summarised in
Chapter~\ref{chap:further} rather than reproduced, and each summary opens with
the thesis author's role.

\section{A layout with a price need not be left to the learning rule}
\label{sec:intro_proposal}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Four things are established by this point. The learning rule decides the
layout. The layout can be predicted from the rule's update equation. The rule
holds the layout in place rather than locking it in, so it goes on paying for
it against the pull of the gradient for as long as training runs. And the
layout charges for itself in one of two currencies, robustness or adaptability.

Read together they make the usual arrangement look indirect. If a spread layout
of mutually supporting neurons is what buys tolerance to damage, then obtaining
one by selecting a learning rule, and having that rule hold it against the
gradient, is a long way round. The short way is to build the layout into the
architecture and leave learning to fill in its parts.

Chapter~\ref{chap:cna} is a first implementation of that
proposal~\cite{sager_cooperative_2025}. The Cooperative Network Architecture
represents a sensory pattern by a net, a set of neurons each of which is
supported by several others through recurrent connections, assembled from
fragments learned by Hebbian plasticity~\cite{hebb_organization_1949}. Support
is a structural property of the representation and not something the training
was asked to produce, and three consequences follow from it without being
optimised for. A neuron stays active only while the pattern around it is one
the network has seen often, so noise, which has no support, is filtered even
when more than half of the input is corrupted. A missing part of a figure
receives support from its surroundings and is completed. Fragments learned on
straight lines recombine into digits, characters and line drawings that never
appeared in training.

The implementation does not yet scale beyond line drawings, uses fixed feature
detectors rather than learned ones, and needs a hand set attenuation schedule.
It is therefore a proposal with a first implementation and not a finished
alternative. The chapter states those limits and lists what a representation of
this kind would need in order to serve as the state of an agent's model of its
environment.

\section{Research questions}
\label{sec:intro_questions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The chain above is organised by four questions.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Is the layout of a learned solution fixed by the task, or by the
    procedure that trained the network? Chapters~\ref{chap:grokking}
    and~\ref{chap:attention} answer it by varying one part of the procedure at
    a time with everything else held fixed, the learning rule in the first case
    and the training objective in the second.
    \item Can the layout be predicted from the procedure without running it,
    and can it be chosen? Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} derives the prediction
    from the update equation and tests it on published optimizers, and
    Chapter~\ref{chap:attention} does the same for the training objective.
    \item What does a layout cost? Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} proves and
    measures the trade between tolerance to deletion and speed of adaptation.
    \item Given that the rule decides the layout and the layout has a price,
    can the layout be fixed by construction instead, and what would that buy?
    Chapter~\ref{chap:cna} answers with a proposal and a first implementation.
\end{enumerate}

Chapter~\ref{chap:acu} supplies the two gaps that make the first three
questions worth asking. Chapter~\ref{chap:bounded} supplies the measurement
discipline the answers depend on, and Chapter~\ref{chap:further} two cases of
structure that survives it. Neither tests the four questions, and the thesis
does not claim that they do.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro_contributions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This is a cumulative thesis. Each results chapter is built around one paper,
reproduced as published, and the thesis author's role is stated at the opening
of each chapter. The contributions, in the order the thesis presents them, are
these.

\paragraph{A survey and taxonomy of agents for computer use.} Shared first
authorship with Benjamin Meyer. A taxonomy along the domain, interaction and
agent perspectives, applied to $87$ papers and $33$ datasets, and six research
gaps derived from the classification~\cite{sager_survey_2026}. The reading of
two of those gaps as properties of the trained network, and as the two ends of
one trade, is thesis work and appears in no paper.

\paragraph{The learning rule decides the layout of a grokked solution.} First
authorship. Twelve published rules compared at a matched setting, a criterion
for which rules generalise at all, a per neuron rescaling of credit that moves
the layout while provably leaving the objective and every update direction
unchanged, a law that predicts from a rule's update equation whether it spreads
the solution, and the trade between deletion tolerance and adaptation speed,
proven in a tractable model and measured~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}. The paper
also shows grokking under a control driven learning rule, to our knowledge for
the first time.

\paragraph{The training objective decides the layout of the attention
weights.} Shared first authorship with Matteo Saponati, who developed the
mathematical framework. The thesis author designed and ran most of the
experiments across model families and modalities. Bidirectional training
induces symmetry in the query key form and autoregressive training induces
column dominance, and symmetric initialisation speeds up bidirectional
training~\cite{saponati_structures_2025}.

\paragraph{A protocol for evaluating latent rollouts.} First authorship. A
controlled study in which a bound that wins by five orders of magnitude under
the standard readout is shown to be a property of the readout, the mechanism
measured directly and then removed at the loss, and a short protocol whose
every item is tied to a failure it would have
caught~\cite{sager_bounded_2026}.

\paragraph{The Cooperative Network Architecture.} First authorship. The first
formal definition of nets and net fragments, a computational framework that
learns fragments by Hebbian plasticity, and the evidence that the resulting
representation filters noise, completes figures and recombines fragments into
unseen patterns~\cite{sager_cooperative_2025}. Its reading as a layout fixed by
construction, and the proposal built on it, are thesis work.

\paragraph{Two studies of structure supplied from outside the data.}
Hounsfield unit masks as an input constraint for data efficient CT
segmentation, shared first authorship with Benjamin Meyer, with the thesis
author responsible for the framing rather than the
implementation~\cite{meyer_hounsfield_2025}. A hybrid retrieval pipeline for
scientific source retrieval, first authorship~\cite{sager_deep_2025}. In the
benchmark of efficient language models the thesis author ran the model
evaluation~\cite{tuggener_private_2024}, and in the compositional reasoning
framework the thesis author evaluated one of the baseline
models~\cite{cogitao}.

\paragraph{The frame.} The definition of layout, the chain that connects the
grokking, attention and Cooperative Network Architecture results, the reading
of the survey's gaps as properties of the trained network, and the proposal of
Part~\ref{part:construction} are the thesis author's work and appear in no
paper.

\section{Structure of the thesis}
\label{sec:intro_structure}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Chapter~\ref{chap:background} gives the background the later chapters rest on.
It covers the set of parameter settings that fit a given task and what is known
about how a procedure selects among them, sparse and distributed coding and the
measures that quantify it, grokking and the circuits found in it, credit
assignment rules other than backpropagation and the optimizers that differ in
what they normalise, self attention as a bilinear form, the assemblies the
closing proposal descends from, and the evaluation of latent rollouts.

The five parts that follow are the argument of
Sections~\ref{sec:intro_agents} to~\ref{sec:intro_proposal} in order.
Part~\ref{part:agents} establishes the two gaps and shows why a score cannot
explain them. Part~\ref{part:procedure} varies the learning rule and then the
training objective. Part~\ref{part:measurement} supplies structure from outside
the data and shows what has to be controlled when doing so.
Part~\ref{part:construction} builds the layout into an architecture.
Part~\ref{part:synthesis} states what the task fixes and what the procedure
fixes, what follows for the design of learning rules and architectures, and
what the thesis leaves open.

Every paper backed chapter has the same shape. A box gives the full citation
and the status of the published paper. A contribution statement names the
co-authors and the thesis author's share. A chapter introduction, written for
this thesis, connects the previous chapter's result to this one and gives the
mapping between the paper's notation and the thesis notation where they differ.
The body of the paper follows as published. A chapter summary, written for this
thesis, states the chapter's findings once in full and hands off to the next
chapter. The thesis frame uses British spelling, and the imported chapters keep
the spelling they were published with.

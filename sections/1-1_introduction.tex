A network trained to add two numbers modulo a prime, with $2048$ neurons in
its hidden layer, solves the task perfectly and carries the computation on
about $332$ of them. The rest contribute almost nothing. Changing one thing in
how the network is trained, namely what the update treats as a group when it
normalises, takes that number to $2046$ out of $2048$. The task is the same,
the data are the same, the architecture and the width are the same, and test
accuracy falls by about two points, from $0.998$ to
$0.976$~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}.

So how much of a network ends up doing the work is not settled by what the
network is asked to do. It is settled by how the network is trained, and more
precisely by how training divides the error among the neurons. This thesis is
about that, about what it costs, and about what else is left open in a trained
network once the task has had its say.

\section{A trained network uses some of its neurons and not others, and the task does not set which}
\label{sec:intro_question}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The two ends of that range fail differently, which is why the question is
worth asking. A computation spread over many neurons survives the loss of any
one of them, because none of them carries much on its own. A computation held
on a few leaves the rest of the network idle, so there is more to prune away
and more room to put to a new use. The second effect holds under a condition
and the condition belongs with it. A concentrated network adapts roughly twice
as fast to a task that needs new combinations of the features it already has,
and not to a task that only changes which answer each feature votes
for~\cite{sager_grokking_2026}.

Machine learning has mostly assumed that the task answers the question. The
clearest case is grokking, where a network fits its training data perfectly,
sits at chance on held out data for thousands of epochs, and then generalises
abruptly~\cite{power_grokking_2022}. For addition modulo a prime the
generalising solution has been taken apart neuron by neuron: the embedding
maps each operand onto a few frequencies and the hidden layer combines them
into the sine and cosine of the sum~\cite{nanda_progress_2023}. That solution
uses few of the available neurons, and the strongest reading of the
observation is universality, the claim that networks solving the same task
find the same features and the same construction~\cite{chughtai_toy_2023}.
Universality is a claim about what is computed, and nothing here contradicts
it. What is worth noticing is that every network behind the evidence was
trained with backpropagation and an adaptive optimizer, which is one procedure
among many, so the evidence cannot separate what the task requires from what
that procedure produces.

The object measured throughout needs a name. The set of neurons that carry a
computation, counted as the participation ratio of a layer's outgoing weight
norms, is what this thesis calls the solution's \emph{circuit}. The
participation ratio of a set of non negative numbers is the square of their
sum divided by the sum of their squares, meaning a count of how many of them
effectively contribute: it equals the number of neurons when all contribute
equally, and falls towards one when a few dominate. Two things about the word.
It is narrower here than in mechanistic interpretability, where a circuit is a
subgraph of components rather than a count of participating units. And the
measure is borrowed from the neuroscience literature on how far a
representation spreads over a population, a borrowing that
\cref{sec:bg_sparsedistributed} states along with the three respects in which
the analogy fails.

Training a network involves a set of choices, and this thesis varies them one
at a time, so they are worth separating. There is the target function and the
data drawn from it. There is the loss written to get the network there. There
is the architecture and the width. There is the computation that assigns each
weight its share of the error at the output, the \emph{credit assignment
algorithm}, of which backpropagation is one. There is the procedure that turns
those shares into a change of the weights, such as stochastic gradient descent
or Adam, which is the \emph{optimizer}. The pair of the last two is the
\emph{learning rule}. And there is the scale at which the weights start.

Each results chapter holds most of that list fixed and moves one entry.
\Cref{chap:grokking} fixes the target function, the data, the architecture and
the width, and varies the learning rule. \Cref{chap:attention} fixes
backpropagation and varies the loss, which in a decoder is realised partly by
a mask over which positions may attend to which, so the architecture moves
with it, which that chapter states. \Cref{chap:bounded} varies nothing in
training and asks instead whether the measurement applied afterwards reports
what it claims. \Cref{chap:cna} gives up the list and fixes the structure in
the architecture.

What credit assignment means here needs a word, because the thesis is examined
where the term usually means how an error signal reaches a synapse. The
condition \cref{chap:grokking} derives compares the credit a neuron receives
against the part of its own update that does not come from the gradient. All
three such terms the chapter unifies are supplied by the optimizer: a weight
decay term added to the gradient, the numerical floor in an adaptive method's
denominator, and the decay of a trainable gain. The quantity the condition is
about is therefore set jointly by the backward pass and by whatever the
optimizer shares a normaliser over, and an account that gives credit
assignment to the backward pass alone cannot state the condition at all. The
cost of using the term this way should be said plainly. Of the rules compared
in \cref{chap:grokking}, exactly one assigns credit by something other than
backpropagation, which is deep feedback control. Feedback alignment, its
direct form and sign based descent do not generalise on the task and are
excluded. Everything else that is varied is an optimizer.

The question did not begin in any of this. It began with agents for computer
use, systems that carry out tasks on a computer or a phone from an instruction
in natural language, by controlling software through clicks, keystrokes and
gestures. A survey of $87$ such agents and $33$ datasets finds six gaps that
recur across the field, four of which concern how the field measures what it
builds and the conditions it builds for~\cite{sager_survey_2026}. Two concern
what training produced, which is where this thesis starts, and \cref{chap:acu}
gives the sort in full. \Cref{chap:further} summarises four further papers,
two of which sharpen those gaps and two of which supply structure from outside
the data.

\section{The learning rule sets how much of a network carries the computation}
\label{sec:intro_rule}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Modular addition earns the detailed work because it is one of the few settings
where the answer can be read off a trained network directly. The algorithm is
known, so counting how many neurons implement it measures something whose
meaning is not in doubt, and the network is small enough that every neuron can
be examined.

A neuron stays in use when the credit it receives is large relative to the
part of its update that does not come from the gradient. That one condition
covers three mechanisms which had no common account before. Equalising credit
across neurons expands the circuit from $60\%$ to $99\%$ of the layer at width
$256$, and switching the intervention on after the network has already
generalised produces the same expansion, so the rule holds the circuit open
for as long as training runs. The price is two points of test accuracy, from
$0.998$ to $0.976$, with margin and parameter norm unchanged to within a few
percent.

The intervention changes a property of the update, so on its own it leaves
open whether the circuit followed the credit or something that travels with
it. The control that closes this imposes a credit profile directly and uses a
random permutation, drawn once per run, to decide which neuron receives which
magnitude, so the assignment has nothing to do with what any neuron is
computing. Circuit participation follows the imposed value, from $0.637$ to
$0.990$, tolerance of neuron deletion rises with it, and test accuracy stays
above $0.96$.

Two limits belong beside this result rather than after it. The first is width.
The effect grows as the layer gets wider, and at width $32$ there is nothing
to do, because the untouched network already uses $30$ of its $32$ neurons and
the intervention reaches $31$. The second is the reference setting, and it is
the sharper limit. That reference adds an L2 penalty to the gradient before an
adaptive optimizer, a combination whose side effects have been known for
years, and the paper says the setting is there to test the condition rather
than as a recommendation. Under decoupled weight decay, which is what current
training code uses, the reference network is already distributed in most of
the places the chapter looks. What survives is the condition, which is
general, and two of its three non gradient terms are live under decoupled
decay: an adaptive optimizer's numerical floor dominates in a wide transformer
whose normalisation parameters are decayed, and so does the decay of a
trainable gain.

What the circuit costs is measured in four currencies and only one of the four
is a surprise. A computation spread over more neurons tolerates random
deletion better, and a network with more neurons left idle has more to prune,
and both follow from what the measure counts. Test accuracy falls by the two
points above. The surprise is transfer, where the concentrated network reaches
a new task about twice as fast, largely by recruiting the neurons it was not
using, under the condition stated at the top of this chapter.

\section{The objective, the readout and the architecture settle three further properties}
\label{sec:intro_others}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The learning rule is not the only component that leaves structure behind. With
backpropagation held fixed and the training objective varied, the update to a
Transformer's attention weights is a sum over whichever ordered pairs of
tokens the objective admits. Bidirectional training enters each pair in both
orders and the weights stay close to symmetric, while autoregressive training
makes a token the target once and the context of every later token, and a few
columns come to dominate~\cite{saponati_structures_2025}. The measured object
there is a column of a bilinear form rather than a count of neurons, so this
is a second component settling a second property and not a second measurement
of the first. What it adds is scale, in models far larger than the grokking
setting reaches.

A third property is left free by the loss altogether. A latent world model is
scored by fitting a probe from latent space to the physical state on encoded
observations and then applying it to predicted latents. Where the training
loss compares directions and ignores magnitudes, nothing fixes the magnitude,
and during a rollout the norm of a predicted latent drifts while its direction
stays accurate. The probe responds to the norm~\cite{sager_bounded_2026}.
Rescaling each prediction to a reference norm taken from the encodings, which
needs no retraining, removes most of the probe's error.

That chapter sets a standard for readouts, and the standard is the one thing
in this thesis that crosses chapters. Its four checks apply to the
participation ratio, which is itself a readout taken from weight norms, and
the discussion runs them. The answer is partly uncomfortable: the measures
tied to the computation place the untouched network lower than the structural
one does, so the direction of the result survives and the level does not. One
piece of evidence is clean, which is that an optimizer normalising per neuron
reaches a distributed circuit while holding each neuron's incoming weight norm
fixed, so its result cannot be scale moved between a neuron's incoming and
outgoing weights. Beyond that standard nothing is shared. No single
measurement runs across the chapters, and two that were planned are not
available, because no checkpoints survive from the attention work and the code
behind \cref{chap:cna} no longer runs.

The last chapter asks what it would mean to stop leaving the structure to
training. The Cooperative Network Architecture represents a sensory pattern as
a set of neurons each of which is held in place by several others, assembled
from fragments learned by Hebbian plasticity. Noise, which nothing supports,
is filtered even when more than half the input is corrupted, occluded figures
are completed, and fragments learned separately recombine into patterns the
network never saw~\cite{sager_cooperative_2026}. It reaches by construction
the redundancy of support that a learning rule can be made to produce by
training, and it reached it first: that work was submitted in $2024$ and the
grokking work is from $2026$, so the two lines meet at a mechanism rather than
one following from the other. It does not scale beyond line drawings, and the
chapter is offered as a proposal.

\section{Research questions}
\label{sec:intro_questions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What sets how much of a trained network's width carries the
    computation? (\cref{chap:grokking})

    \item Which rules change it, and can that be read off the form of their
    update? (\cref{chap:grokking})

    \item What is given up when a computation is spread over more neurons, in
    test accuracy, in tolerance of neuron deletion, in how far the network
    prunes and in how fast it transfers? (\cref{chap:grokking})

    \item Does a second component of the training setup leave its own
    structure in a trained network's weights? (\cref{chap:attention})

    \item Does a readout used to make claims about a trained network report
    the quantity it names, or one the loss left free? (\cref{chap:bounded})

    \item Does a structural measure of a trained network report the structure,
    or a quantity the training procedure left free? (\cref{chap:bounded}
    and~\cref{chap:grokking})

    \item What does an architecture that builds in redundancy of support
    achieve without training it, and where does it stop? (\cref{chap:cna})
\end{enumerate}

Question $6$ is the only one that two chapters bear on. It costs no
experiments, because it applies the method of one chapter to the measure of
another, and it is the question a reader with both chapters open will ask
first.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro_contributions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{itemize}
    \item A survey and taxonomy of agents for computer use, covering $87$
    agents and $33$ datasets and identifying six recurring gaps. Shared first
    authorship with Benjamin Meyer. \Cref{chap:acu}.

    \item The finding that the learning rule, and not the task, sets how much
    of a network's width carries a grokked solution, together with the
    condition that decides it, an intervention that moves it across its range,
    and a measurement of what the move costs. First authorship.
    \Cref{chap:grokking}.

    \item A derivation of the structure that each of the two pretraining
    objectives leaves in a Transformer's attention weights, checked in trained
    models at scale, with a symmetric initialisation that follows from it.
    Shared first authorship, led by Matteo Saponati. \Cref{chap:attention}.

    \item The finding that the standard probe for latent world models responds
    mostly to a quantity the training loss leaves free, and a correction that
    needs no retraining, with four checks on what the corrected score reports.
    First authorship. \Cref{chap:bounded}.

    \item An architecture in which a sensory pattern is represented by
    mutually supporting neurons assembled from Hebbian fragments, filtering
    noise, completing occluded figures and recombining learned fragments into
    unseen patterns. First authorship. \Cref{chap:cna}.

    \item Four further studies, summarised rather than reproduced: a benchmark
    of efficient language models, a framework for compositional generalisation,
    domain knowledge supplied in the input of a segmentation model, and a
    retrieval system that holds its knowledge in an index. Roles are stated per
    paper. \Cref{chap:further}.

    \item Three contributions of the frame itself. The vocabulary above. A
    sort of the survey's six gaps by what each one is a property of. And the
    application of one chapter's standard for readouts to another chapter's
    measure, which is the only argument in this thesis that runs between two
    chapters.
\end{itemize}

What is left if the grokking chapter is removed is worth saying, because a
cumulative thesis invites the question. The claim about circuits would go with
it, since that chapter is where it is established. What would remain is an
independent structural result about attention weights in models at scale, a
methodological correction to how latent world models are scored, a survey that
sets the stake, and an architecture that reaches the same mechanism by another
route. That is a body of work and it is not this thesis's argument. The
argument has one pillar, and saying so is more useful than implying five.

Five chapters reproduce a published or submitted paper. Each opens with the
full citation and a statement of the thesis author's share, then a chapter
introduction written for this thesis, then the paper's own text as it was
published, then a chapter summary. The imported text keeps the spelling,
notation and figure numbering it was published with, so a reader will find
American spelling inside those chapters and British spelling around them, and
where a chapter's notation differs from the thesis's the chapter introduction
gives the mapping. A citation in the margin is a work at its first mention in
a chapter, and the bibliography carries all of them in full.

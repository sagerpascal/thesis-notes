A network trained to add two numbers modulo a prime, with $256$ neurons in its
first hidden layer, reaches a test accuracy of $0.998$ and carries the
computation on about $154$ of them. Changing one thing in how it is trained,
namely which parameters share a normalising factor in the update, takes that
to about $254$, with the task, the data, the architecture and the width
unchanged, and test accuracy falls to $0.976$. The difference grows with the
layer: at $2048$ neurons the untouched network uses $332$ and the changed one
$2046$, and there both reach $0.998$. How much of a network ends up doing the
work is therefore not settled by what the network is asked to do. It is
settled by how training divides the error among the neurons.

The question comes from a survey of $87$ agents for computer use and $33$
datasets. The field is converging on one recipe, a pretrained model with
vision and language capabilities adapted by prompting or by cloning human
demonstrations, and six limitations recur across it. Three concern how the
field measures what it builds and the conditions it builds for, and one, poor
planning over many steps, the survey places partly in the model and partly in
an environment whose dynamics are unknown. Two concern what training produced,
namely that these systems generalise poorly beyond the situations they were
trained on and need large amounts of demonstration data.
A survey cannot explain those two, because it never holds a task fixed and
varies how a network is trained, and because a success rate cannot separate
two networks that score alike. They have to be measured inside the network.

Doing that needs a task whose solution is known neuron by neuron, and
arithmetic modulo a prime is such a task. Networks trained on it exhibit
grokking, meaning that they fit the training data perfectly, sit at chance on
held out data for thousands of epochs, and then generalise abruptly. Holding
the task, the data, the architecture and the width fixed and varying only the
rule that assigns credit, every rule that generalises recovers the same
Fourier construction, and the number of neurons implementing it differs by
more than half again between rules.

One condition accounts for this. A neuron stays in use when the credit it
receives is large relative to the part of its own update that does not come
from the gradient, which covers three mechanisms that had no common
explanation before: a weight decay term added to the gradient, the numerical
floor in an adaptive optimizer's denominator, and the decay of a trainable
gain. Only a normalising factor shared by exactly one neuron can change that
ratio, which places the optimizers in general use on one side of a line and
two rules on the other. Equalising the credit neurons receive, while leaving
every gradient's direction unchanged, moves the computation from $60$ to $99$
per cent of the layer and costs the two points of accuracy above. Imposing a
credit profile directly, with a random permutation deciding which neuron
receives which magnitude, moves it the same way, which rules out the
possibility that the credit merely followed what the neurons were already
doing. The effect grows with the width of the layer and vanishes where the
untouched network already uses all of it.

The choice is paid for in both directions. A computation spread over many
neurons tolerates the deletion of neurons far better. A computation held on
few prunes further and reaches a task that needs new combinations of the
features already learned in $640$ epochs against $1540$ for the spread one,
which is no faster than the $1410$ needed from scratch. The advantage is
narrow: it does not appear for a task that only changes which answer each
feature votes for, and on multiplication no pretrained network beats training
from scratch at all.

The learning rule is not the only part of training that leaves structure
behind. Holding the credit assignment algorithm fixed and varying the training
objective, the update to a Transformer's attention weights is a sum over the
ordered pairs of tokens the objective admits. Bidirectional training enters
each pair in both orders and the weights stay close to symmetric, while
autoregressive training makes a token a target once and context many times,
and the weights become directional, meaning that columns of high norm come to
outnumber rows of high norm. The derivation holds in matched
twelve layer models and the structure appears in released models across text,
vision and audio. An initialisation that follows from the derivation speeds up
bidirectional training.

A measurement of a trained network is only as good as the readout behind it,
including this thesis's own. The standard probe for a latent world model is
fitted on encoded observations and applied to predicted ones, and where the
training loss compares directions and ignores magnitudes, nothing fixes the
magnitude. The probe then reports mostly the drift in it: in synthetic data
its error reaches $27$ times that of predicting the average state, and for a
published model rescaling the predictions to the norm of the encodings removes
$97$ per cent of the error without retraining anything. Four checks come with
the correction, and applying them to this thesis's own measure of how far a
computation spreads shows that the direction of the central result survives
while the level it reports is the highest of three measures.

A structure that training settles can instead be built in, and one
architecture did so before any of this was measured. The last study, submitted
two years before the work above, represents a sensory pattern as a set of neurons each held in place by several
others, assembled from fragments learned by Hebbian plasticity. Noise, which
nothing supports, is filtered with more than half the input corrupted,
partially hidden figures are completed, and fragments learned separately
recombine into patterns never seen in training. The model does not scale
beyond line drawings, so the thesis closes by setting out what a
representation of this kind would need in order to serve as the state of an
agent's model of its environment.

Two networks can fit the same data equally well and still be built differently
inside. One carries its computation on a few of its neurons and leaves the rest
idle. The other spreads the same computation over all of them. Both reach the
same training loss, so nothing in the data chooses between them. This thesis
asks what does, and finds that the training procedure does. The choice can be
read off the procedure's update equation before the procedure is run, it is
paid for in either robustness or adaptability, and it can be designed instead
of inherited.

The question comes from a survey of $87$ agents and $33$ datasets for
computer use. The field builds those agents by one recipe, a pretrained
foundation model adapted by prompting or by behaviour cloning, and the agents
share six gaps. Four concern the benchmarks. The other two, insufficient
generalisation and inefficient learning, are properties of the trained network.
A survey cannot explain them, because the field never varies the training
procedure and because a success rate cannot separate two networks that score
alike. Two shorter studies confirm how little a score reveals, one finding that
quantisation sold as nearly lossless costs text quality that raters notice and
metrics miss, the other that architectures built for composition still fail on
rule combinations they have not seen. The property has to be measured inside
the network instead.

Measuring it there needs a task whose solution is known neuron by neuron, and
grokking on modular arithmetic is such a task. Holding task, data, architecture
and optimizer fixed and varying only the learning rule, four of twelve
published rules never generalise, because their update does not vanish at the
minima of the loss and so cannot come to rest where gradient descent does. The
rules that do generalise reach the same loss, the same margin and the same
weight norm, and they spread the solution over anywhere from $154$ to $254$ of
$256$ neurons. The task offers every rule the same set of solutions, and each
rule stops at a different member of it.

Rules differ in many ways at once, so the cause is isolated with a rescaling of
each neuron's share of the error that provably changes neither the objective
nor any neuron's update direction. It moves the count across the full range,
applied from the first step or switched on after the network has already
generalised. Which published rules move it follows from their update alone,
because a normaliser shared by several neurons rescales them together and
cannot alter the inequality between them. Only per neuron normalisation can,
and that prediction places all eight optimizers tested. The choice is not free.
Random deletion costs every solution the same expected fraction of its output,
so what separates solutions is the fluctuation around that average, which
shrinks as one over the root of the effective neuron count. Spread solutions
survive deletion far better, and concentrated ones adapt to a new task about
twice as fast.

Small networks and a purpose built intervention are thin evidence, so the same
question is put to a component the field does vary, in models at scale. With
backpropagation held fixed and the training objective varied, the update to a
Transformer's attention weights is a sum over the token pairs the objective
allows. Autoregressive training makes each token the context of every later
token and a target only once, so a few columns absorb most of the update.
Bidirectional training takes every pair in both orders, so the update is
symmetric and the structure spreads. Both appear in ModernBERT, GPT, LLaMA3 and
Mistral, and across text, vision and audio. Symmetric initialisation follows
from the derivation and speeds up bidirectional training, and autoregressive
prediction is the objective behind every foundation model the survey found.

A procedure that fixes a property the data leaves open creates a hazard,
because a measurement sensitive to that property can be satisfied with nothing
learned. This thesis ran into it. Planning over several steps, the survey's
third gap, needs a model that predicts several steps ahead, and the standard
remedy bounds the spectrum of its transition operator. The bounded operator
wins by five orders of magnitude under the readout this literature uses, and
two free controls overturn that. Predictors that learn no dynamics beat every
trained model, and a readout blind to latent scale erases the gap. The loss is
scale invariant, so training never fixes that scale, the bound fixes how fast
it grows, and the probe, fitted at the training scale, measures exactly that. A
bounded error curve is not a better model. Every result above is therefore
reported at matched loss, margin and weight norm and against predictors that
learn nothing. Two further studies supply structure from outside the data and
survive those controls, one with input masks from tissue specific Hounsfield
ranges, the other with an external index for scientific source retrieval.

The learning rule therefore decides how the solution is laid out, holds that
layout against the gradient for as long as training runs, and charges
robustness or adaptability for it. Choosing a rule is a roundabout way to
obtain a spread layout, and building one in is the direct way. The Cooperative
Network Architecture represents a sensory pattern as a net, a set of neurons
each supported by several others, assembled from fragments learned by Hebbian
plasticity. Because support is structural rather than trained, noise, which has
none, is filtered even when more than half of the input is corrupted, occluded
figures are completed, and fragments learned on straight lines recombine into
digits and characters that never appeared in training. The model does not yet
scale beyond line drawings, so the thesis closes by setting out what a
representation of this kind would need in order to serve as the state of an
agent's model of its environment.

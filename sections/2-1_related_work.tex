\section{Many parameter settings fit the same data, and the procedure picks one}
\label{sec:bg_solutionset}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: establish the solution set, and that the training
% procedure rather than the task selects a member of it. This is the premise
% the whole thesis rests on, so it comes first.

\subsection{An overparameterised network has many settings that fit}
\label{sec:bg_interpolation}

A network with more parameters than training examples can fit its training
data exactly. It can do so even when the labels are random, which means that
no property of the data stands in the way of memorisation and that reaching
zero training loss says almost nothing about which function was
found~\cite{zhang_understanding_2017}. Fitting is not the constraint.

What fits is therefore a set and not a point. Many parameter settings
reach the same training loss, and they differ in properties the loss never
sees: how large the weights are, how wide the margin is, how the computation
is arranged across the network's parts. Classical accounts of generalisation
were built for the regime in which that set is small, and the behaviour of
modern networks past the interpolation threshold does not fit
them~\cite{neyshabur_search_2015, belkin_reconciling_2019}. This thesis is
about one property that the loss leaves open, which is how many of a network's
neurons end up carrying the computation.

\subsection{The training procedure selects among them}
\label{sec:bg_implicitbias}
% The term to introduce here, in plain words, is implicit bias: a preference
% among the settings that fit, produced by the procedure rather than by the
% objective.

If the objective does not single out one setting, something else does. The
preference that training shows among the settings that fit, produced by how
the network is trained rather than by what it is asked to compute, is what the
literature calls implicit bias.

Sometimes that preference can be stated exactly. On data a linear model can
separate, gradient descent on the logistic loss sends the weights to infinity,
and the direction it converges to is the maximum margin
solution~\cite{soudry_implicit_2018}. The objective is indifferent between
separating directions, and the procedure is not.

Which property gets minimised depends on the geometry of the update rather
than on the objective. Steepest descent under different norms, and mirror
descent under different potentials, reach different minima of the same
loss~\cite{gunasekar_characterizing_2018}. Changing the update is changing
which member of the solution set is reached.

The scale at which the weights are initialised moves a network between two
regimes. Small initialisation leaves the features free to move and the network
learns them, while large initialisation keeps the network close to its
linearisation and it behaves like a kernel
method~\cite{woodworth_kernel_2020}. The two regimes reach different
solutions, and the transition between them has been connected to the delay
between fitting and generalising that this chapter turns to
below~\cite{kumar_grokking_2024}.

The optimizer changes the solution and not only the speed of reaching it.
Adaptive methods and plain gradient descent, trained to the same training
loss, reach solutions that generalise
differently~\cite{wilson_marginal_2017}.

\subsection{Some of the choice is visible in what the update conserves}
\label{sec:bg_conserved}
% This subsection is the machinery the grokking chapter's propositions use, so
% it has to be here rather than in that chapter.

Part of what a procedure will and will not do to a network can be read off its
update without solving the dynamics. Under gradient flow on a network built
from homogeneous layers, the difference of squared norms across a layer
boundary does not change~\cite{du_algorithmic_2018}. Whatever imbalance the
initialisation put there survives training.

The general statement is that every continuous symmetry of the
parameterisation gives a quantity the dynamics cannot
alter~\cite{kunin_neural_2021}. Rescaling a unit's incoming weights and
dividing its outgoing weights by the same factor leaves the function
unchanged, and permuting units does too, and each such freedom pins something
in the flow. Weight decay and momentum break these conservation laws, and they
break them in ways that can be written down.

The consequence this thesis uses is about ratios. If the flow conserves the
ratio between two units' contributions, then no amount of training can reshape
the relative sizes the network started with, and a rule that conserves their
difference instead can. \Cref{chap:grokking} turns that observation into a
condition on the update.

\subsection{It has asked about norms, and not about how many units carry the computation}
\label{sec:bg_biasgap}

The properties this literature characterises are global. Norm, margin, rank
and effective dimension are all properties of the whole parameter vector, and
a network can be described completely in those terms without any statement
about which of its units are doing the work. The closest the literature comes
to a structural claim is the bias of deep matrix factorisation towards
solutions of low rank, which is a statement about the spectrum and not about
the parts~\cite{arora_implicit_2019, gunasekar_characterizing_2018}.

The part of the procedure these results vary is also narrow. They vary the
optimizer, or the geometry of the update, or the scale of the initialisation.
The computation that assigns each parameter its share of the error stays at
backpropagation throughout. That is the opening this thesis works in: a
property the literature has not asked about, and a component of the procedure
it has not varied.


\section{Credit assignment algorithms and optimizers have been compared on convergence and accuracy}
\label{sec:bg_rules}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: give the reader the rules Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking}
% compares, organised so that the organising property is visible. The
% vocabulary (credit assignment algorithm, optimizer, learning rule) is defined
% in Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction} and used plainly here.

\subsection{Credit can be assigned without backpropagation}
\label{sec:bg_credit}

Backpropagation computes each weight's share of the output error by sending
the error backwards through the transpose of the forward
weights~\cite{rumelhart_learning_1986}. A biological circuit has no obvious
way to hold that transpose, which is the objection that sent people looking
for alternatives~\cite{crick_recent_1989}.

Random feedback turned out to be enough. Routing the error through a fixed
random matrix still trains a network, because the forward weights move into
rough alignment with that matrix instead of the other way
round~\cite{lillicrap_random_2016}, and the direct form delivers the error to
every layer at once instead of passing it down the
chain~\cite{nokland_direct_2016}. The alignment happens early in training and
what follows it is largely memorisation of the training
set~\cite{refinetti_align_2021}.

A second family leaves the path alone and changes the geometry. Natural gradient
preconditions the update by the curvature of the loss in function
space~\cite{amari_natural_1998}, and a Kronecker factored approximation makes
that affordable for a deep network~\cite{martens_optimizing_2015}. At the
other extreme the sign methods throw the magnitude away and keep only the
direction of each coordinate, which converges under conditions that separate
the plain sign update from one that carries an error feedback
term~\cite{bernstein_signsgd_2018, karimireddy_error_2019}, and a search over
such update rules produced one that is in current
use~\cite{chen_lion_2024}.

A third family replaces the backward pass with a process that settles.
Predictive coding networks approximate backpropagation with local error units
when the network relaxes to equilibrium~\cite{whittington_approximation_2017},
equilibrium propagation reads the gradient off the difference between two
settled states~\cite{scellier_equilibrium_2017}, target propagation sends
targets backwards in place of errors and corrects the inversion
error~\cite{lee_difference_2015}, and the forward-forward algorithm replaces
the backward pass with a second forward pass on contrasting
data~\cite{hinton_forward_2022}. Deep feedback control drives the network
towards the target with a controller and reads the credit off the control
signal, which makes the resulting update interpretable as an approximation to
a known optimisation
method~\cite{meulemans_credit_2021, meulemans_least_2022}.

What every one of these is asked is the same question. Does it match the
accuracy of backpropagation, and does it converge? Which member of the
solution set it selects is left open, and none of them has been run on a task
whose solution is known at the level of single neurons. The closest case is a
kernel method that reproduces the delayed generalisation of grokking without
any backward pass at all, and a kernel method has no per neuron structure to
read~\cite{mallinar_emergence_2025}.

\subsection{Optimizers differ in how many parameters share one normaliser}
\label{sec:bg_grain}
% Present this as one spectrum in one property, because that property is what
% Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} predicts from.

The optimizers in general use can be laid along a single axis: how many
parameters share one normalising factor.

At one end each weight gets its own. Adam keeps a running estimate of the
gradient's second moment per parameter and divides by its square
root~\cite{kingma_adam_2015}, RMSProp does the same with a simpler
average~\cite{tieleman_lecture_2012}, Adafactor stores that estimate in
factored form to save memory~\cite{shazeer_adafactor_2018}, and Lion reduces
the whole thing to the sign of an interpolated
momentum~\cite{chen_lion_2024}.

Further along, one factor covers a layer or a whole matrix. Layerwise trust
ratios rescale every weight in a layer by one scalar derived from the ratio of
weight norm to gradient norm~\cite{you_batch_2017, you_batch_2020}. Shampoo
preconditions a weight matrix with factored second moment
matrices~\cite{gupta_shampoo_2018}, and Muon orthogonalises the momentum
matrix before the step~\cite{jordan_muon_2024}. In each case the transformation
is shared by many neurons at once.

At the other end sits a normaliser shared by exactly the weights entering one
neuron. Nero normalises the update of each neuron's incoming weights and
constrains their norm~\cite{liu_learning_2021}. Weight normalisation looks
similar and is not: it reparameterises those weights into a direction and a
length and leaves the update itself alone~\cite{salimans_weight_2016}, so it
does not rescale a neuron's gradient against the other terms of its update.
\Cref{chap:grokking} measures both, and only the first distributes.

These methods are usually compared on convergence speed and final accuracy,
and the axis above is not what they are usually sorted by. It is the axis
\cref{chap:grokking} sorts them by.

\subsection{What is known about these rules is convergence and accuracy}
\label{sec:bg_rulesgap}

Each of the rules above arrives with its own convergence analysis and its own
table of benchmark numbers. What does not exist is a comparison that holds the
task, the data, the architecture and the width fixed, varies the rule, and
then reads the solution each rule reached at the level of single neurons. That
comparison is what would separate a property of the task from a property of
the procedure, and it is the comparison \cref{chap:grokking} makes.


\section{In modular addition the grokked solution has been read neuron by neuron}
\label{sec:bg_grokking}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: explain the phenomenon, explain that its circuit is
% known at the level of single neurons, and end on the assumption the thesis
% tests. Gloss "grokking" inline at first use.

\subsection{Generalisation can arrive long after the training data is fitted}
\label{sec:bg_grokphenomenon}

On small algorithmic tasks such as arithmetic modulo a prime, a network
trained with weight decay on full batches reaches perfect training accuracy
within a few hundred epochs and then sits at chance on held out data for
thousands more, before test accuracy climbs abruptly to one. The behaviour is
called grokking, meaning that generalisation arrives long after the training
data has been fitted~\cite{power_grokking_2022}.

The explanations offered for it differ in what they take to be the slow
quantity. One account makes it the weight norm, with generalisation arriving
once the network has travelled far enough from its
initialisation~\cite{liu_omnigrok_2022}. Another makes it efficiency, with a
memorising circuit and a generalising circuit competing under a regulariser
that eventually favours the second~\cite{varma_explaining_2023}. A third
places it on the axis between the kernel and the feature learning regimes,
where the delay is the time the network takes to leave the first for the
second~\cite{kumar_grokking_2024}. An effective theory of representation
learning describes the same transition in terms of the structure that forms in
the embedding~\cite{liu_understanding_2022}, and there is evidence that progress is
continuous underneath and only the standard metrics are
flat~\cite{barak_hidden_2022}.

Closest to this thesis is the finding that the optimizer decides whether
grokking happens at all, and when. Adaptive methods produce a cyclic
instability around the transition that changes its
timing~\cite{thilak_slingshot_2022}. That result varies a component of the
procedure and reads the consequence in the training curve. It does not ask
which of the solutions that fit was reached.

\subsection{For modular arithmetic the circuit is known neuron by neuron}
\label{sec:bg_grokcircuit}

What makes this setting useful is that the generalising solution has been
taken apart. Each operand is embedded onto a handful of frequencies, trigonometric
identities in the hidden layer turn the pair into functions of the sum, and
the output unit for every candidate answer peaks at the right
one~\cite{nanda_progress_2023}. The same computation has been derived in
closed form for a network of one hidden layer~\cite{gromov_grokking_2023}, and
the analysis carries over to composition in a group that is not commutative,
where the network uses the group's irreducible representations in place of
frequencies~\cite{chughtai_toy_2023}.

A margin analysis of a one hidden layer model sharpens the picture and bundles
two properties together. The solutions it describes have neurons that each
tune to a single frequency, and they have a computation carried by many
neurons at once~\cite{morwani_feature_2024}. These are separate properties.
What each neuron represents is one question, and how many neurons the
computation is spread over is another, and this thesis is about the second.

Even with backpropagation held fixed, the circuit is not unique. A different
algorithm appears under other hyperparameters, one that composes rotations
instead of reading off a sum~\cite{zhong_clock_2023}. A sparse generalising
subnetwork can be separated from the dense memorising network around
it~\cite{merrill_tale_2023}. And a small initialisation drives a network into
a condensed regime in which few effective neurons remain active at
all~\cite{luo_phase_2021}.

\subsection{The assumption is that the task fixes the circuit}
\label{sec:bg_universality}

Running through this work is the assumption that the task settles the answer.
Its strongest form is universality, the claim that networks solving the same
task converge on the same features and the same construction, so that an
account read out of one network stands for the
task~\cite{chughtai_toy_2023, olah_zoom_2020}. The word circuit is used in that
literature for a subgraph of components, which is not the count this thesis
measures, and the two senses are kept apart here.

The claim operates at two levels that the evidence does not separate. One is
what is computed, and for modular addition the answer is the Fourier
construction above. The other is how many neurons carry it. This thesis does
not contest the first. What the evidence for the second shares is that all of that evidence comes from networks trained one
way, by backpropagation under an adaptive optimizer.


\section{How far a computation spreads has been measured in neuroscience, and the measures disagree}
\label{sec:bg_coding}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: supply the measure the thesis uses, and the evidence
% that the spread has consequences. The neuroscience framing is the declared
% analogy of Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction} and is kept to one subsection.
% This section used to open the chapter. It was moved here on 2026-11-16,
% when the spine changed to credit assignment, because what the thesis takes

\subsection{The question comes from neuroscience, and so does the measure}
\label{sec:bg_sparsedistributed}

Whether a stimulus is represented by a few highly selective cells or by a
pattern of activity over many is among the oldest questions about neural
representation. The first position has a clean statement in the proposal that
perception is built from the responses of cells of increasing
specificity~\cite{barlow_single_1972}, and the second in the argument that a
concept is better held as a pattern over many units, each of which
participates in many concepts~\cite{hinton_distributed_1986}.

Sparsity can be derived and need not be assumed. Optimising a linear code of
natural images for sparse activity yields filters that are localised, oriented
and bandpass, which is what receptive fields in primary visual cortex look
like~\cite{olshausen_emergence_1996}, and the argument has been developed as a
general principle for sensory coding~\cite{olshausen_sparse_2004} and realised
in learning rules that produce sparse
representations~\cite{foldiak_sparse_1995}. Cells of remarkable specificity do
exist: single neurons in the human medial temporal lobe respond to one person
across photographs, drawings and the written
name~\cite{quianquiroga_invariant_2005}. That is evidence for one end of the range
and not a settlement of the question.

The relation between that literature and this thesis is an analogy, and where it fails should
be said before the measure is borrowed from it. Sparse
against distributed coding is a claim about how a population represents
stimuli, measured over an ensemble of stimuli, where the quantity this thesis
measures is structural and is read off the weights. The mechanisms that
concentrate a computation in \cref{chap:grokking} are a coupled weight decay
term, an optimizer's numerical floor and the decay of a trainable gain, none
of which has a counterpart in a brain. And classical sparse coding derives
sparsity from an explicit prior in an efficient coding objective, where here
it is an unintended consequence of a regulariser. What this thesis takes from
the literature is the measure, and it does not claim to model a brain.

\subsection{Each end of the range buys something and gives something up}
\label{sec:bg_codingprice}
% This subsection is the one the thesis leans on hardest, because the trade off
% it describes is the one Chapter~\ref{chap:grokking} measures.

A representation carried by many cells degrades gently when some of them are
lost. If a signal is summed over many carriers, removing a few changes the sum
little, and the fluctuation around the average shrinks as the number of
carriers grows, which is the standard population coding
argument~\cite{averbeck_neural_2006}. A representation carried by few cells
buys something else. Fewer shared cells means that storing something new
disturbs less of what is already there, and capacity arguments favour sparse
connectivity for the same
reason~\cite{rolls_relative_1990, litwinkumar_optimal_2017}.

Both effects appear in artificial networks, where they have been studied under
other names. Removing parameters by their effect on the loss is an old
compression technique~\cite{lecun_optimal_1989} and remains the basis of
modern pruning~\cite{han_learning_2015}. How much a trained network depends on
single directions in activation space varies, and depending on few of them
goes with worse
generalisation~\cite{morcos_importance_2018, zhou_importance_2018}. Sparse
subnetworks exist inside dense ones that train to full accuracy on their
own~\cite{frankle_lottery_2019}. And redundancy can be bought deliberately, by
dropping units at random during training~\cite{srivastava_dropout_2014} or by
imposing sparse high dimensional activity by
design~\cite{ahmad_can_2019}.

\subsection{Participation ratio counts the parts that contribute, and some measures mislead}
\label{sec:bg_measures}

The quantity this thesis measures is the participation ratio, defined in
\cref{sec:intro_question} and read off a layer's outgoing weight norms. It is
standard in both
literatures~\cite{litwinkumar_optimal_2017,gao_simplicity_2015}.

A different question is sometimes asked with similar words. How many features
one unit carries, and whether a network packs more features into a layer than
it has units, is the subject of superposition and
polysemanticity~\cite{elhage_toy_2022, bricken_monosemanticity_2023}. That is
not the same as how many units carry one computation, and the two should not
be run together.

The measure has to be chosen with care, because some quantities that look like
measures of spread depend on the basis they are computed in. Spectral entropy
of a weight matrix is one. A measure taken over the units of a layer inherits
whatever freedom the parameterisation leaves in the size of a unit's weights,
which is why \cref{chap:grokking} reports measures tied to the computation
alongside the structural one, and why the two do not always agree.


\section{The two pretraining objectives differ in which token pairs enter the update}
\label{sec:bg_attention}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: get the reader to the object Chapter~\ref{chap:attention}
% studies, which is the product of the query and key matrices, and to the fact
% that the two objectives differ in which token pairs enter the update.

\subsection{Query and key act only through their product}
\label{sec:bg_qk}

A Transformer layer scores every pair of positions in its input and mixes the
positions according to those scores~\cite{etal_attention_2017}. Each token's
embedding is mapped to a query and to a key, the score for a pair is the inner
product of one token's query with another's key, and a third map produces the
value that is mixed. Up to the softmax taken over each row, the score is a
bilinear function of the two embeddings.

That is why the object worth studying is the product of the query and key
matrices rather than either of them. The two never appear separately in the
score, so any structure visible in one alone can be moved into the other by a
change of basis, while their product acts on the embeddings as a single
bilinear form~\cite{elhage_mathematical_2021}. Reading that product directly,
rather than the attention maps it produces, has been used to interpret what a
head attends to~\cite{dar_analyzing_2023}.

\subsection{The two pretraining objectives differ in which pairs enter the update}
\label{sec:bg_objectives}

Bidirectional pretraining hides some of the tokens and asks the model to
recover each from the whole surrounding sequence~\cite{devlin_bert_2019}, a
recipe still in use in current
encoders~\cite{warner_smarter_2024}. Every pair of positions therefore enters
the computation in both orders: each is at some point the masked target and at
some point part of the context.

Autoregressive pretraining asks for the next token given its predecessors
only~\cite{radford_improving_2018}, which is the objective behind the models
that followed~\cite{brown_language_2020, touvron_llama_2023a}. Here a token is
the target exactly once and is context for every token after it, so a pair
enters in one order.

Stated neutrally, the two objectives define different sets of ordered token
pairs, and the update to the attention weights is a sum over the pairs the
objective admits. \Cref{chap:attention} works out what that difference leaves
in the trained weights.

\subsection{Reading structure off attention weights has been attempted from the outside}
\label{sec:bg_interp}

The usual approach is to interrogate a trained network. Probing fits a light
classifier on frozen features and asks whether some property is decodable from
them~\cite{belinkov_probing_2022}, and the control task literature shows how
readily a probe's success overstates what the model itself
uses~\cite{hewitt_designing_2019}.

Reading the attention distribution as an explanation is unreliable. The
distribution can often be changed substantially without changing the model's
output, which undercuts its use as an account of what drove a
prediction~\cite{jain_attention_2019}, although what follows from that depends
on what the explanation is wanted for~\cite{wiegreffe_attention_2019}.

A more recent line recovers features and circuits. Sparse autoencoders pull
interpretable features out of activations that are individually
uninterpretable~\cite{huben_sparse_2024, bricken_monosemanticity_2023}, circuit
analysis identifies subgraphs of a network that implement a specific
behaviour~\cite{olah_zoom_2020}, induction heads are the best understood
example~\cite{olsson_context_2022}, and feedforward blocks have been read as
key value memories~\cite{geva_transformer_2021}.

All of this works from the activations of a network that has already been
trained. None of it derives what structure a training objective must leave in
the weights, which is the opening \cref{chap:attention} works in.


\section{A world model is scored through a probe, and the probe's own behaviour has rarely been checked}
\label{sec:bg_supplied}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: cover the three side chapters of Part IV at background
% depth, and set up the one lesson that part carries. Keep it tight. Each of
% those papers has its own related work; this section gives only what the
% frame's argument needs.

\subsection{A learner needs a bias, and it can be placed at several points}
\label{sec:bg_inductive}

No procedure generalises from finite data without a preference among the
hypotheses that fit it. Averaged over all problems no learner beats any
other~\cite{wolpert_free_1997}, so the question is never whether to have a
bias but which one, and the classical statement of the cost is the trade
between bias and variance~\cite{etal_neural_1992, mitchell_need_1980}.

The bias can be placed in the architecture. Sharing weights across positions
makes a network equivariant to translation~\cite{etal_gradient_1998}, and the
general treatment derives architectures from the symmetries of the domain they
act on~\cite{bronstein_geometric_2021}.

It can be placed in the input or the loss instead. Known physical law can be
written into the objective rather than left to be discovered from
data~\cite{raissi_physics_2019}, and the same idea applied to a medical image
is the subject of \cref{sec:hounsfield}.

Or it can be supplied at inference. Retrieval grounds a prediction in an
external index rather than in the weights~\cite{lewis_retrieval_2021}, which
scales to very large stores~\cite{borgeaud_improving_2022}, and the standard
components are lexical matching~\cite{robertson_probabilistic_2009}, dense
retrieval against learned embeddings~\cite{karpukhin_dense_2020} and a cross
encoder that rescores the shortlist~\cite{nogueira_passage_2019}.
\Cref{sec:retrieval} is a system of that kind.

\subsection{A latent world model predicts forward, and its errors compound}
\label{sec:bg_worldmodels}

A latent world model encodes an observation, applies a learned transition in
that latent space, and never reconstructs the observation
itself~\cite{lecun_path_2022}. Training the encoder to predict its own
representations rather than pixels is the self supervised version of the same
idea~\cite{assran_self_2023}, and models of this family are what current model
based agents plan
with~\cite{hafner_mastering_2023, schwarzer_data_2021}.

Rolling such a model forward composes the transition with itself. Each
application can magnify whatever error is already present by its Lipschitz
constant, so over a horizon of many steps the error can grow without bound.

The standard remedy constrains the transition. If the latent dynamics are
linear with a bounded spectral radius, the product of Lipschitz constants
collapses into a bound that does not grow with the
horizon~\cite{lusch_deep_2018, azencot_forecasting_2020, mamakoukas_memory_2020}.

\subsection{Scoring a rollout needs a readout, and the readout responds to a free quantity}
\label{sec:bg_scoring}

Because nothing in the latent space is directly interpretable, a rollout is
scored through a probe: a regressor fitted from latent to physical state on
encoded observations, then applied to predicted
latents~\cite{belinkov_probing_2022, hewitt_designing_2019}.

A prediction loss computed in latent space need not fix the scale of that
space. Where the loss compares directions and ignores magnitudes, the
magnitude is free, and the collapse phenomena studied in self supervised
learning are what a free direction in the representation can
do~\cite{grill_bootstrap_2020, chen_exploring_2021, jing_understanding_2022}.

The control that catches a readout reporting the wrong quantity is a predictor
that learns nothing. Several fields have found headline results that a trivial
baseline matches once someone ran
one~\cite{ferraridacrema_really_2019, musgrave_metric_2020, lin_neural_2019}, and in
each case the response was a protocol rather than a better
model~\cite{henderson_deep_2018}. That is the standard this thesis holds its
own comparisons to.

\subsection{An assembly is a set of neurons that support each other}
\label{sec:bg_assemblies}

The oldest proposal in this lineage is that neurons which are active together
strengthen the connection between them, so that a set of mutually supporting
neurons becomes the unit in which something is
represented~\cite{hebb_organization_1949}. Support is structural: a neuron is
part of the representation because other neurons hold it there.

Associative memory made the idea precise and, in doing so, rigid.
Strengthening the connection between every pair of neurons active in a pattern
stores that pattern as a whole~\cite{amari_learning_1972}, and retrieval
becomes descent on an energy function towards the nearest stored
state~\cite{hopfield_neural_1982}. What is stored is a complete snapshot
rather than a set of parts that can be recombined, and capacity holds only
while the stored states overlap little.

Stochastic recurrent networks gave the same machinery a learning rule.
A Boltzmann machine settles into configurations that satisfy its constraints
and is tuned by a rule comparing two
phases~\cite{etal_boltzmann_1984, smolensky_information_1987}, which became
practical only once the connectivity was restricted so that one phase could be
sampled in closed form~\cite{hinton_reducing_2006}.

\subsection{Dynamic links, nets and the binding problem}
\label{sec:bg_binding}

Correlation theory proposed that a representation is carried by connections
that are temporarily selected rather than by activity
alone~\cite{malsburg_correlation_1981}, and the later development of that
line describes a representation as a net assembled from reusable
fragments~\cite{malsburg_concerning_2018}.

The difficulty these proposals address is binding. A layer of feature units
that reports which features are present cannot say which of them belong to the
same object, so two objects sharing a scene become one ambiguous
description~\cite{etal_binding_2020}, and the perceptual grouping literature
has studied how the pieces of a scene get assigned to objects in the first
place~\cite{etal_century_2012}.

Deep learning has its own structured alternatives. Capsules replace a unit's
scalar activation with a vector that carries the pose of a part and route
information between levels by agreement between
predictions~\cite{hinton_transforming_2011, sabour_dynamic_2017}. They share
with the assembly tradition the idea that a representation has parts standing
in relations, and they stopped short of scaling to the inputs that
convolutional networks handle routinely.

\subsection{Where these models stop}
\label{sec:bg_substratelimits}

Three limits run through the whole lineage. The features these models compose
are hand designed rather than learned from data. The demonstrations are on
small, often synthetic inputs. And although these models have dynamics by
which a representation settles, none defines a transition over the world being
represented, so there is nothing in them to plan with. \Cref{chap:cna}
inherits all three.

\section{Unused neurons have been described one mechanism at a time, and never as a quantity a rule sets}
\label{sec:bg_gap}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
% Job of this section: name the gap and stop. Half a page. No results, no
% roadmap paragraph, no repetition of the contributions list from
% Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction}. One clause per part at most.
%
% The earlier title claimed no literature had asked how many neurons carry a
% computation. That is false, and the grokking manuscript's own related work
% names the literature it is false against. The claim this section makes now
% is the one that paper makes: the routes are known separately and have not
% been unified or read as a quantity a rule selects.

Neurons that end up carrying nothing have been described several times over,
each time as one mechanism. An L2 penalty added to the gradient before an
adaptive optimizer drives whole units and filters to
zero~\cite{yaguchi_adam_2018, mehta_implicit_2019}. The numerical floor in that
optimizer's denominator lets units with very small second moment estimates
die~\cite{dufortlabbe_maxwell_2025}. A decayed per unit gain acts as a gate
that induces sparsity~\cite{mehta_implicit_2019, kolb_differentiable_2025}.
In large language models trained with a matrix
preconditioner, neurons whose rows are updated by persistently small amounts
simply die~\cite{dewulf_aurora_2026}. And under coupled decay, where a neuron
settles depends on its own gradient norm~\cite{kosson_rotational_2024}. Each
of these is treated as a pathology to be avoided or as a lever for pruning.

What has not been done is to treat them as one thing. There is no account that
derives a single condition covering the separate routes, and none that reads
the number of neurons left in use as a property of the rule, to be compared
across rules at a matched setting and priced. The closest work asks how
grokking relates to the structure a network settles
into~\cite{manningcoe_grokking_2025}, which is a question about the phenomenon
rather than about the rule.

Three literatures above come close from other directions and none of them asks
it either. \Cref{sec:bg_solutionset} varies the optimizer and characterises
which norm or margin the procedure selects, never which units are in use.
\Cref{sec:bg_coding} supplies a measure for how far a computation spreads and
evidence that the answer has a price, in populations of cells rather than in a
trained layer. \Cref{sec:bg_grokking} supplies the one setting where the
answer can be read off a network neuron by neuron, with the procedure held
fixed throughout.

So three specific things are missing. A comparison that holds the task, the
data, the architecture and the width fixed, varies the rule that assigns
credit, and reads the solution each rule reached. A statement of which rules
change the number of neurons in use, derived from the form of their update
rather than found by trying them. And a measurement of what the resulting
circuit costs.

Two smaller ones are missing too. The same question has not been asked of a
Transformer's attention weights with the training objective as the variable.
And the assembly tradition has not been read as a case where the structure is
fixed by construction rather than produced by training.
